## What It Is

In this July 2025 essay, Jez calls the convergence of financial insecurity and easy access to high-risk bets “long degeneracy.” The argument: when wages and patient saving feel insufficient to reach housing and asset ownership, people seek asymmetric upside through crypto, options, meme stocks, sports betting, and other forms of what the author calls “hypergambling.”

The piece connects that financial behavior to a wider social picture: loneliness, online communities replacing in-person ones, and a sense that AI is shortening the time available to get ahead. These are the author's claims and predictions, not settled outcomes.

## Why It Matters

The useful question here is what people do in the near term when the conventional social contract—work, save, build a stable life—no longer looks credible. The essay traces consequences beyond investment portfolios: risk appetite, attention, community, and the widening gap between winners and losers.

## When To Use It

Use this as a lens when thinking about housing affordability, speculative finance, crypto adoption, gambling products, and the social effects of an uncertain economic future. Pair it with other evidence before treating its predictions as forecasts.
